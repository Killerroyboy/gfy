Pick the frame it deserves.

The fist (Candidate F, from your reference photo) is untouched in all three — same path, same authority. What changes is everything around it: weight, type, scenery, attitude. All three are drawn embroidery-honest: no hairlines, nothing thinner than thread.

Where we are

Good fist, timid frame. The pine boughs read as fish bones, the rings are hairlines thread can't stitch, the type whispers, and half the badge is dead air. Nothing below keeps any of it except the fist and the words.

AThe Park BadgeWPA poster · McCall scenery · my recommendation

The joke wears a ranger uniform. Payette Lake, the ridgeline, real pines, a moon — a legitimate Idaho heritage badge in every detail, except the thing rising out of the lake is your fist. Reads sincere at ten feet, lands at three.

Dense, flat, three inks — this is drawn the way patch embroiderers want art delivered. The moon disc exists to silhouette the fingertip; the scenery gives the crest a place, which is what the current one is missing.

BThe Club Crestmaximum pomp, minimum respect

Augusta pomp around a middle finger. Regimental pinstripes on the shield, crossed driver and iron, laurels, a dimpled golf ball where the heraldic crest goes, and a hand-lettered scroll delivering the club motto. EST MMXIX because Roman numerals make everything more disrespectful.

Densest of the three and the hardest "holy shit" on first read. More parts to embroider — it wants to be a woven patch or a chest crest on a polo more than a flat cap stitch.

CThe Rope-Hat Patch70s trucker · loudest · simplest

Zero subtlety, maximum hat. Two-tone field, rope-hat borders, three-letter arch, one huge fist. This is the one strangers can read across a parking lot, and the one a patch shop could stitch tomorrow without simplifying anything.

The trade: it says GFY loud but tells the least story — no lake, no pines, no country-club joke. Pure gear energy.

Recommendation

A — the Park Badge. It keeps the site's quiet-menace tone, gives the crest an actual place (the lake, the ridge, the pines you drive past to get there), and it's the one that looks better the longer you look — which is what you want on a hat you'll wear for years. B is the funniest single image; C is the most legible gear. All three stitch. Happy to iterate on any of them, or cross-breed (A's scene with C's weight, B's scroll under A's badge…).
